# Decisions

Every important choice we made, in plain words. Each one says **what we chose**, **what we didn't choose**, **why**, and **when to rethink it**.

Numbers never change (the code refers to some of them, e.g. "DECISIONS.md #8"). New decisions go at the bottom.
For how the app works end to end, see [FLOW.md](FLOW.md).

### 1. A website that works like an app (PWA), not a Flutter app
- **We chose:** a PWA. It's a website you can "install" on the home screen, and it keeps working without internet.
- **Instead of:** a Flutter Android app (what the proposal said).
- **Why:** Judges can open a link on any phone: no APK file, no Play Store, no installing. It can still record audio and work offline, which is all we need.
- **What it means for us:** Visits are stored inside the browser (IndexedDB), not in a phone database. Uploading happens while the app is open, not silently in the background.
- **Change it when:** a real pilot needs background uploads or deeper phone features.

### 2. Frontend: React + Vite + plain JavaScript + Tailwind
- **Why:** These are the fastest tools to build with, and easy for either of us to read. Tailwind keeps the styling right inside each screen's code.
- **Instead of:** TypeScript (slower to write in a short build) or Next.js (we don't need its extra features).

### 3. Backend code layout: routes → controllers → services, plain functions
- **We chose:** three layers, each with one job:
  - **routes** receive the web request
  - **controllers** do the feature's logic and the database work
  - **services** talk to outside companies (Claude, Sarvam)
- **Why:** You always know where to look. We write plain functions, not classes, so the code is quick to pick up.
- **What it means for us:** The only classes are simple data shapes (Pydantic models) and error types.

### 4. One small server: FastAPI + SQLite
- **We chose:** one Python server that serves both the app and the data, with SQLite (a database that's just one file).
- **Instead of:** Postgres or other big databases, which need setup and add nothing for a demo.
- **What it means for us:** One thing to deploy and one link to share. Data resets when we redeploy, so demo data loads on every start.
- **Change it when:** we need more than one server, or data that survives redeploys.

### 5. Sarvam turns voice notes into text
- **Why:** It's the cheapest option for a demo: new accounts get ₹100 free, about 2 hours of audio. It understands Tamil, Hindi and Marathi, and there's no AI model for us to host.
- **Instead of:** Bhashini (free but slow to get access to), or running AI4Bharat's model ourselves (needs an expensive GPU).
- **Change it when:** we go to a real pilot. Bhashini fits a government project well.

### 6. Claude Opus 5.5 writes the coaching packet
- **Why:** We have $500 of Claude credits. Claude writes well in Indian languages, and it can be forced to answer in an exact format, so the app never gets a half-broken packet. It can also remember (cache) our long reference material between calls, which makes repeat calls cheaper and faster.
- **Settings:**
  - If Claude refuses a request, another model automatically takes over.
  - We never send settings this model rejects.

### 7. The AI's reference material goes straight into the prompt, with no search database
- **We chose:** to give Claude the whole knowledge pack every time: the checklist, 16 practices, the government's grade goals and 21 official quotes.
- **Instead of:** a search database that picks relevant pieces ("RAG").
- **Why:** The pack is small enough to send in full. Claude caches it, so this stays cheap. It's simpler, with nothing extra to run.
- **Change it when:** we add whole state handbooks and the pack becomes too big.

### 8. Voice notes are at most 30 seconds
- **Why:** Sarvam's simple upload accepts clips under 30 seconds. Short notes also force clear, specific observations. The mentor can record as many notes as needed.

### 9. No login for the demo (replaced by #25)
- **Why:** Login isn't what we're judged on, and it would cost a day. The teacher's page is protected by a hard-to-guess link instead.
- **Change it when:** real people use it. Then we need CRP, teacher and block-officer accounts.

### 10. Coaching stays private; the block officer sees only totals
- **Why:** Teachers fear inspection. If the boss could read the coaching, teachers would stop trusting it.
- **What it means for us:**
  - The block view shows only counts like "14 of 22 schools need support: Children reading for themselves".
  - Any group smaller than 5 schools is merged into "other", so nobody can guess which school it was.
  - There are no names, notes or scores.

### 11. Check quality with an AI grader first, experts later
- **Why:** We can't recruit education experts in a week. We'll test 50 made-up visits and have Claude grade each packet:
  - Is it specific, with one priority?
  - Can it be done in 5 minutes?
  - Is it backed by sources?
  - Is it respectful and in the right language?
- **Change it when:** mentors connect us with real practitioners.

### 12. All data is made up
- **Why:** No real child audio and no personal data. Schools, teachers, numbers and notes are invented. Test voice samples come only from adults who agreed.

### 13. Advice is backed by real quotes from official documents
- **What:** Dhruv collected the official documents: NIPUN Bharat guidelines, NCF-FS 2022 and the KVS FLN monitoring booklet. From these we built:
  - **the checklist:** the KVS checklist items you can actually see in one lesson, plus 2 we added ("children read themselves", "pair/group work")
  - **16 five-minute practices**, adapted from the official suggested activities
  - **21 short quotes**, each with its page number
- **How we know the quotes are real:** a script (`backend/scripts/check_grounding.py`) checks that every quote appears word for word on its cited page. As of 25 Oct, all 21 pass.
- **What it means for us:**
  - The app only needs the quotes file (`knowledge/grounding.json`), not the 55 MB of PDFs, which stay in Dhruv's `edu-hackathon-fln` folder.
  - Page numbers are PDF page numbers, which can differ from the printed ones.
  - The Hindi, Marathi and Tamil checklist text still needs a native speaker to check it.
- **Instead of:** Claude's built-in citation feature, which can't be combined with the fixed answer format we need.

### 14. Dhruv's bigger design is kept for later
- **What:** Dhruv's `APP_PLAN.md` describes a production-scale system: a bigger database, a separate background worker, two-way sync and logins.
- **Why not now:** It's too much for this build. We keep the simple version (#4) but reuse its best ideas: the checklist/practice/quote design, its safety rules, and the "at least 5 schools" privacy rule (#10).
- **Change it when:** a real pilot with many users and phones. Then APP_PLAN is the blueprint ([futurescope](improvements/futurescope.md)).

### 15. Claude picks from fixed lists; the server fills in the rest
- **What:** Claude must choose **one checklist item** to improve and **one practice** from our library, by their ids. The server then adds:
  - the **need area** (looked up from the checklist item)
  - the **source pages** (looked up from the quote ids)
- **Why:** If Claude typed these freely, the block view's counts could drift ("reading" vs "Reading skills") and citations could be invented. Lookups can't drift.
- **What it means for us:**
  - If Claude names something that doesn't exist, the server sends it back for one more try. If that fails too, the visit shows "Failed".
  - Never rename a checklist item id; add a new one instead, or old packets break.

### 16. Reviewing works offline; approving needs internet
- **Why:** The mentor often reads the packet while still in the school, with no signal, so edits are saved on the phone as they're typed. But approving creates the teacher's link, which has to be stored on the server, so it waits for a connection.
- **What it means for us:**
  - After approval the packet is locked.
  - The mentor can edit only the parts the teacher will read, not the evidence or sources.

### 17. Each voice note is transcribed once; typed notes skip Sarvam
- **Why:**
  - If writing the packet fails and the mentor presses Retry, the notes already turned into text are kept, so we don't pay Sarvam twice.
  - A visit with **only a typed note** never calls Sarvam. That's our backup plan for the demo if Sarvam is down.

### 18. Deadline is 1 Nov; features freeze on Thu 29
- **Why:** The real deadline is Sun 1 Nov; we earlier planned for 25 Oct by mistake. We keep two spare days:
  - **Fri 30:** quality checks
  - **Sat 31:** submit
  - **Sun 1 Nov:** left free in case something goes wrong
- **Update (1 Nov):** submission is on Mon 2 Nov ([SUBMISSION.md](SUBMISSION.md)). The features in #25–#31 were built after the planned freeze, so the click-through in SUBMISSION.md section 1 matters more.

### 19. The teacher's text holds only what the teacher should read
- **What happened:** In the first real packet (25 Oct), Claude put a note for the CRP inside the teacher's text: *"[CRP: add an example here]"* in "What went well". The CRP approved it, and approving locks the packet, so the teacher would have seen it.
- **We chose:**
  - **Teacher's parts** are in the teacher's language: strength, priority, practice, materials, demo, agreed action and check. They contain nothing addressed to the CRP.
  - **CRP's parts** are in English and plain words: the summary and the evidence. They use checklist wording, never code ids like `children_read`.
  - **A safety net:** Approve is blocked, on the phone and on the server, while a `[CRP: …]` note is left anywhere in the teacher's text.
  - **Wording:** neutral (we don't know the CRP's gender, so no "बुलाऊँगा/बुलाऊँगी"), no children's names even as examples, and only the 2–4 sources that really support the advice.
- **Why:** The teacher must get clean, respectful advice, and the CRP must never be tricked into sharing a draft.
- **Status:** built on 26 Oct. The prompt rules still need a check on real Claude output.

### 20. Only a teacher's latest agreed action is followed up, and it works offline
- **We chose:**
  - At the next visit, the app asks about the action from the teacher's **most recent** approved visit only. Older unchecked actions are out of date once there's a newer packet.
  - The mentor's answer (done / partly / not done, a note, and how useful the teacher found the advice, 1–5) is saved on the phone in an **outbox** and sent when there's signal.
  - Each visit keeps one answer, so sending it twice is harmless, and the mentor can change it.
- **Why:** The next visit is in a school without internet too, so the question must be on the phone before the mentor arrives. That's what the **offline pack** is for: the phone downloads every school's open actions every couple of minutes while online.
- **Also:** The answer goes into Claude's next packet for that teacher ("agreed: pair reading → partly done"), so the advice builds on what happened.
- **Usefulness rating:** asked by the mentor at the next visit, because the teacher has no app to rate in.

### 21. The app is saved on the phone with a ready-made PWA plugin
- **We chose:** `vite-plugin-pwa`. When the app is built, it adds a service worker that keeps a copy of every app file on the phone, plus a manifest and icons so the app can be installed.
- **Instead of:** writing our own service worker. That's more code to get right, for no benefit.
- **What it means for us:**
  - It only works in the **built** app (`npm run build`, or the deployed site), not in `npm run dev`, so test offline behaviour on the built app.
  - Data requests (`/api/...`) always go to the server; only the app's own files are kept.

### 22. Demo history is built from our practice library, not generated by Claude
- **We chose:** the server creates the demo data itself on first start:
  - 22 schools
  - two past visits for one teacher per school, with packets made from the practice library
  - checked follow-ups for the older visits
- **Why:** It's free, gives the same result every time, and needs no API key. The block view only needs counts, and the next-visit card only needs the agreed action. Those action lines are written in each school's language (to be checked by native speakers). The rest of a demo packet says "Demo data" in English.
- **Change it when:** we want realistic old packets to open in full. Then generate them once with Claude and save them as a file.

### 23. Rehearsal sends the whole conversation each turn and stores nothing
- **Why:** Each turn is one standalone call: the packet plus the conversation so far, as text. That's simple, nothing is saved on the server, and there's no long chat state to manage. A rehearsal is capped at 16 lines.
- **What it means for us:** Rehearsal needs internet. Closing the page ends the rehearsal.

### 24. The teacher's WhatsApp message is sent from the mentor's own WhatsApp, not through Twilio or the WhatsApp API
- **We chose:** after approving, the mentor taps **Send on WhatsApp**. That opens WhatsApp on the mentor's phone with the message already written: the advice in Marathi plus the link. The mentor picks the teacher and presses send. (Technically a `wa.me/?text=…` link.)
- **Instead of:** our server sending the message through Twilio or Meta's WhatsApp Cloud API.
- **Why:**
  - The message comes from a **person the teacher knows**, not an unknown business number. That fits "mentoring, not monitoring" (MENTOR.md #1, #11).
  - **No setup and no cost:** no Meta business verification, no approved message templates, no per-message charges.
  - **We store no teacher phone numbers.** The mentor's phone already has them.
  - The Twilio test mode ("sandbox") only reaches people who first text a join code to Twilio's number. It's fine for a test, but not something to show the state as the real flow.
- **Change it when:** the state wants automatic messages (reminders before the next visit) or a WhatsApp bot for mentors (futurescope, phase 2). Then use a state-owned number on the WhatsApp Business API, through Meta directly or a provider such as Twilio.

### 25. Simple login with demo users; teachers still never log in
- **We chose:** a username and password per person, checked by our own server. Four demo users are created on first start and shown on the login screen:
  - `sachin` / `sachin123` (mentor)
  - `vandana` / `vandana123` (mentor)
  - `officer` / `officer123` (block officer)
  - `admin` / `admin123` (state admin, added with #28)
- **How it works:**
  - Passwords are stored hashed (PBKDF2, from Python's standard library, so no new package).
  - Logging in gives the phone a random token, which every request sends.
  - Mentors can use everything except the state checklist page. The block officer only sees support planning, and the state admin only sees the state checklist (#28).
  - **The teacher's link needs no login**, as before.
- **Why now:** the mentor's name used to be typed into a pop-up and could be anything. Now each visit is recorded under whoever is logged in, which the coaching history (#6 in MENTOR.md) depends on.
- **Offline:** the first login needs internet. After that the login is kept on the phone, so visits still work with no signal.
- **Change it when:** real users arrive. Then add phone + OTP or state single sign-on, tokens that expire, and accounts created by an admin rather than demo users.

### 26. The mentor's screens switch between English, Marathi and Hindi; packets stay in the teacher's language
- **We chose:** a language switch in the menu (and on the login screen). Every label, button, checklist item, "what good looks like" text, need area and status is translated. All the text lives in one file, `frontend/src/lib/strings.js`.
- **What doesn't change:**
  - The packet and the WhatsApp message are always in the **teacher's** language, whatever the mentor picks.
  - What Claude writes only for the mentor (the summary, the evidence, the rehearsal feedback) is in English, and so are the quotes from the source documents.
- **Why:** half-translated screens are hard to use. A Kendra Pramukh in Maharashtra should be able to use the app fully in Marathi.
- **Change it when:** a native speaker has checked the Marathi and Hindi (they're drafts now). Later, Claude could also write the mentor's summary in the mentor's language.

### 27. Dark mode by flipping the colour palette, not by restyling every page
- **We chose:** a light/dark switch in the menu. It starts from the phone's own setting. In dark mode, `index.css` swaps every colour shade with its mirror (lightest ↔ darkest), so every text and background pair keeps its contrast.
- **Instead of:** adding a `dark:` class to every element on every page, which is hundreds of changes and easy to miss one.
- **What it means for us:** new pages get dark mode for free if they use the usual Tailwind colours. Inside a `dark:` class the colour names are flipped too (used only for the green top bar).

### 28. Each state's checklist is data the state uploads, with versions
- **We chose:** the observation checklist lives in the database, one list per state, with a version number. The **state admin** (e.g. SCERT; demo login `admin` / `admin123`) can:
  - see the version in use
  - download it as CSV
  - edit it in Excel or Google Sheets
  - upload it again as the next version (CSV or JSON)

  Mentors get the newest version on their next sync.
- **Why:** the mentor told us each state already decides its checklist (MENTOR.md #4). A state should be able to change it without asking us to change code.
- **How it stays safe:**
  - A bad file is rejected with every problem listed at once, and nothing changes.
  - Every visit remembers the version it was observed with, so old visits and their packets stay correct.
  - Claude is told the visit's own checklist, and the packet's priority must be one of its items.
- **Kept simple:**
  - The state comes from the user's login, not the phone's location.
  - Items must use our 8 need areas, so support planning can still count them.
  - Maharashtra version 1 is our reading-and-maths list mapped to KPALP.
- **Change it when:** a second state joins (add it to `STATES` in `controllers/checklists.py` and upload its list), or a state wants to review a version before it goes live (add a draft step).

### 29. Notes beside the checklist, and a "Did I miss anything?" check before ending a visit
- **We chose:**
  - **Notes panel.** The mentor's notes sit beside the checklist on a tablet, or in a pull-up drawer on a phone. Voice notes are in the same panel. Tapping 📝 on any checklist item starts a note line about that item. The mentor can also dictate with the phone keyboard's 🎤.
  - **End visit opens a short check:**
    - Unrated items are worked out on the phone, so this works offline.
    - Claude compares the ticks with the typed notes. It reports **ratings the notes contradict** (e.g. "every child gets a turn: Yes", but the notes say only the front rows answered) and **important points the checklist doesn't capture** (e.g. no drinking water, a child who needs extra help).
    - "Go to item" jumps back to fix it, or the mentor ends the visit anyway.
- **Why:** the mentor's job in class is to watch, not to fill in a form perfectly. The check makes sure no critical point is lost.
- **How:**
  - One small Claude call at effort `low` (`POST /api/visits/review`), with nothing stored. It answers in the mentor's screen language.
  - Only ids on the visit's own checklist are kept.
  - Voice notes aren't checked here, because they are transcribed after upload. The visit report covers them.
- **Change it when:** voice notes are transcribed on the phone, so they can be checked too.

### 30. Claude writes the visit report; the mentor checks it and sends it themselves
- **We chose:** after a visit is processed, the mentor opens **Visit report**. Claude writes the parts that need judgement: a summary, key observations, points to check and support needed. It works from the ticks, all the notes (voice notes as transcribed, plus typed ones), the class details, the agreed action and the earlier actions. The facts come straight from the data, so Claude can't get them wrong: school, teacher, date, mentor, every checklist rating and the agreed action. The mentor sends it on **WhatsApp** or **saves it as a PDF**, and it is marked as sent.
- **Why:** after every visit, mentors write a report by hand. That's time taken away from mentoring (MENTOR.md #1). We don't yet know where Maharashtra wants it submitted (MENTOR.md section 4), so we write it and the mentor sends it wherever the process says.
- **Not sent to the block officer's page automatically:** a teacher-level report on an officer dashboard would turn the app into monitoring. Support planning still shows only counts (#10).
- **How:**
  - The report is written on demand (`POST /api/visits/{id}/report`) at effort `low`, in the mentor's screen language.
  - Writing it again, for example in another language, replaces it and clears "sent".
  - It is cached on the phone to open offline.
- **Change it when:** the state names its submission channel (a portal, an email address or a form). Then "send" can go there directly.

### 31. Suggestions for the next checklist version come from the visits themselves
- **We chose:** the state admin's page has a **"What the visits say"** section, based on every visit observed with the version in use. It has two parts.
  - **Rules (no AI), once an item has been seen in at least 10 visits:**
    - items rated "yes" in 90% or more of visits are **almost always met** (remove them, or raise the bar?)
    - items left unrated in half the visits or more are **often skipped** (hard to see in one visit? reword or drop?)
    - the 3 items most often rated "no" are **where teachers need most support** (keep them)
  - **Claude (on request):** it reads the mentors' notes and suggests up to 5 **new items** for problems that keep coming up but aren't on the list. Examples: no drinking water, low attendance at harvest time, children from migrant families who don't follow the teaching language. Each suggestion has the item in English, Marathi and Hindi, the need area, what to look for, and how many notes mention it.
- **What the state does with it:**
  - **Print** the section for the government.
  - Download a **suggested next version** as CSV (the version in use plus the new items), edit it, and upload it (#28).
  - Nothing changes the checklist by itself.
- **Privacy:** only totals and themes come out. Claude is told never to name a school, teacher or child.
- **Demo data:** the made-up history has realistic ratings and recurring notes, so the section has something to show. Print-rich classrooms and home language come out as "almost always met", and "children explain their thinking" as "often skipped".
